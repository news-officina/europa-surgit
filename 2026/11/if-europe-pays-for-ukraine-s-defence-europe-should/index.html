If Europe pays for Ukraine's defence, Europe should build it

Washington has stopped funding new weapons and now wants our money for American-made arms. Borrow together, order together, and turn those euros into factories on our own continent.

18 November 2026

Ukraine's own drones and missiles are hitting Russian oil refineries, military sites and logistics deep inside Russia, aimed at the revenue that funds Moscow's war. Built in Ukraine, and already flying. And they are working, by Washington's own account: at the Ukraine Defence Contact Group in Brussels, United States Under Secretary of War for Policy Elbridge Colby said Ukraine gained more territory than Russia in October, for the second month running, and that Russian forces suffered their highest monthly casualty toll of the war. And here is where we stand: Europe's governments should fund Ukraine's defence through common borrowing spent on joint European procurement and production. Europe pays. Europe builds.

What holds it back is the chequebook and where it points. The United States has stopped directly funding new weapons deliveries, leaving Europe and Canada to finance supplies, including American-made arms. Colby told Europe to take primary responsibility for funding Ukraine's support, including robust funding for PURL, the American-run buying channel through which, as NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte says, the vast majority of Patriots and 75 percent of air-to-air missiles have gone to Ukraine. Washington even commended the EU for procuring US-origin weapons with those funds. The payer is being praised for buying someone else's industry, for a war on Europe's own border. Yet the same Washington warns that Ukraine's deep strikes risk escalation and are not ending the war. Germany's Defence Minister Boris Pistorius called the warning, plainly, 'quite a stupid thing', and British Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said there was 'no doubt' the strikes had helped degrade Putin's war machine, buying time and saving lives. Colby himself called Ukraine's mid-range drone campaign very effective.

The drones Colby praises are Ukrainian, and Kyiv wants to build them with us.

Fund the alternative, and it compounds. Ukraine asks for partners to invest in joint production facilities, and Ukrainian Defense Minister Yevhen Khmara says 62 percent of funding should go to drones: FPV, mid-range, deep-strike, logistics. Give the common borrowing to those lines, and to missile and interceptor plants next to them, and every euro becomes a European job and a machine that outlasts this war. Colby told the ministers Russia appears set to continue mobilising its war economy. A long war needs a long industry. Europe can own it, or rent it from Washington year after year.

Governments and the European Parliament should decide this winter to authorise common borrowing for Ukraine's support, and earmark it for joint orders in which every participating country's industry builds a named share. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy asked the ministers in Brussels for Patriot and IRIS-T interceptors from allies' existing stocks right now. The borrowing can pay for the plants that make the next generation of them here. Pay for the factory, not only the shelf. The earmarked share is what holds the deal together, because the joint orders bring work to each country that joins. Rutte called this meeting the last chance this year to pledge support as Ukraine heads into a hard winter, so the decision cannot wait for spring. Tell your ministers, and your representatives in the European Parliament, that your euros should build this industry on this continent.
