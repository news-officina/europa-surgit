Write the security guarantee before the ceasefire, not after

Moscow's delegates say the continent's security is on the table in Abu Dhabi and Europe is outside the room. The money is approved, the Paris table exists, and all that is missing is our position on paper.

13 November 2026

In Abu Dhabi this week, Russian and Ukrainian delegations sat at the same table for the first time in nine months, since Geneva in February. A war on our continent has reached the stage where Europe's security is being arranged. And where is Europe? Outside the room. Moscow's own delegates say the settlement is merely one item on the agenda and that the real business is reviewing security for the whole continent. That is our security being rewritten while we wait. Europe should write its own security guarantee for Ukraine before the ceasefire happens, not after it. We have the money, we have the governments, we have the format. What we lack is a position on paper, and that is a choice.

What holds us back is our own sequencing. EU officials have ruled that talks on Europe's security arrangements can only start after a ceasefire. That rule was meant to be cautious. Now it is costly. Still outside, and Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov says our statements "don't indicate any desire on their part to contribute to movement along the path toward peace". Sergei Ryabkov, the Russian diplomat who issued an ultimatum to the West weeks before the invasion, sits at that table. The answer to waiting is that a ceasefire negotiated without a European guarantee becomes the framework for everything after it, and frameworks are not reopened. Peskov's own offer is that European representatives could eventually join the talks. But Washington's own envoy, Steve Witkoff, said after the meetings that any future agreements must provide effective security guarantees for Ukraine. The question is whether Europe co-signs it or receives it as a memo.

So we write the guarantee now, while the terms are still open.

Think ten years ahead, to the army that keeps the peace. Whichever cap survives, the 800,000 troops discussed for Ukraine's peacetime army or the US proposal's 600,000, someone must underwrite it. 90 billion euros for 2026 and 2027, already approved. That money becomes a guarantee when Europe co-signs it, and a footnote when it does not. If you are starting your career now, this decides the continent you work in: a war ended under a pledge Europe wrote, or a security sized by others while Europe stands outside the room. The table exists, in Paris. Twenty-seven European governments and Canada met there to plan the defence of Ukraine. The table we need, we built.

Moscow says fewer parties move faster, and that our money speaks louder than our seat: Russia does not want European countries involved in the direct negotiations. It fails for one reason: a guarantee is a promise by those who will have to keep it, and money pledged from the corridor is only honoured once it is written into that promise. The demand, and it has a date. Before the Abu Dhabi round concludes, the Commission and the Council, together with the governments of that coalition, put a common European position on security guarantees for Ukraine on the table, including its multi-year financing. That position is how Europe enters any guarantee framework as a party, and how Ukraine's army is underwritten by Europe, not sized without it. The way past needs no new structure, only a position written for the table that already exists. Hold your governments and your MEPs to one pledge: a guarantee without Europe's signature is not a guarantee.
