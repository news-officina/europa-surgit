Six Nordic defence firms propose one shared procurement chain

Ericsson, Kongsberg, Nokia, Patria and Saab launched the Nordic Compass Defence Track in August, and the six firms now pitch joint missile warning, maritime and digital projects to five governments.

6 November 2026

Ericsson, Kongsberg, Nokia, Patria, Saab and Terma propose to strengthen and develop a space-based joint Nordic missile warning system, enhanced Arctic intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance, and secure, resilient communications linking national, terrestrial and tactical networks. They published the plan in the Nordic Compass Defence Statement at their Gothenburg summit on 5 November.

Patria's chief executive, Panu Routila, put the problem plainly: the Nordic countries spend at record levels and cooperate more than ever. Yet they still run five separate, somewhat differently implemented procurement chains. The firms answered with four first steps: mutual certification, a joint capability roadmap, a buyers' club-backed innovation environment, and a pilot framework contract. That contract pays for production capacity. Routila said the armed forces need capabilities faster and closer cooperation must help deliver them.

In August 2026, Ericsson, Kongsberg, Nokia, Patria and Saab launched the Nordic Compass Defence Track. They are founding drivers of a Defence Track within the Nordic Compass industry Round Table, to strengthen security in Europe. In August 2026, representatives of the five companies met Finnish President Alexander Stubb in Helsinki to discuss industry's role in Nordic defence and security, and the launch took place at the margins of that meeting. Kongsberg CEO Eirik Lie said joining Nordic Compass and closer defence cooperation between the Nordic countries and industry strengthens cross-border defence capabilities. It also, he said, promotes regional security and stability.

The alliance's first initiatives were presented at the Nordic Compass Summit in Gothenburg on 4 and 5 November 2026. Kongsberg CEO Eirik Lie, Terma CEO Henriette Hallberg Thygesen and Patria CEO Panu Routila attended. The six firms pitched a joint space-based missile warning system and a shared maritime picture of surface and subsurface activity to protect critical infrastructure. They also pitched a secure digital backbone. The companies state that these initiatives should complement NATO's and the EU's existing security work.

Patria's Nordic industry model

Patria leads the work on a new Nordic defence industry model. It is one of four Defence Track initiatives, alongside federated space defence, maritime infrastructure protection and a borderless digital backbone. The companies propose one end-to-end Nordic acquisition chain. What is tested, certified or bought in one country would be valid across all participating countries, with national decision rights intact. Terma CEO Henriette Hallberg Thygesen said the Nordic countries face shared security challenges and have complementary defence industries.

The scepticism has a face. Joachim Finkielman said in March that joint Nordic projects still show little concrete on the ground. The common uniform programme, more than ten years in the making and only now being phased in, was his main example. Nordic Compass, the Nordic industry round table led by Finland's former Prime Minister Jyrki Katainen, was launched on 12 May 2026 and gathers 30 leading Nordic companies, foundations and organisations. It covers four tracks, Defence among them.

The ministers back that direction. Finland's defence minister Antti Häkkänen said in September that the goal is at least a joint Nordic, or Finnish-Swedish, defence industry that guarantees supply security, technological know-how and export access. His Swedish counterpart Pål Jonson shares the aim. In late August, Sweden said it would send JAS 39 Gripen fighters and a Visby-class corvette to help Finland monitor its border. Stray Ukrainian drones had crossed in. Häkkänen called the help a natural step towards peacetime cooperation in all disruption situations, and one that eases the load on Finnish personnel.

Häkämies chairs the track

The Nordic Compass Defence Track is chaired by Jyri Häkämies, Finland's former Defence Minister. In late August, Häkämies had already framed the moment as an opportunity. The increased defence budgets, he said, present a significant opportunity for the Nordics, home to globally competitive defence and technology firms with diversified capabilities. They allow new possibilities, he said, while increasing regional resilience.

The companies issuing the invitation now address the governments of Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden, above all their defence authorities and defence forces. The machinery to receive it may already exist, a Finnish government working group assesses. It finds the government-to-government function can be built on existing structures, with the operating model in use by June 2027 at the latest.
