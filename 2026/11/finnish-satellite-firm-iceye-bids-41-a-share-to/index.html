Finnish satellite firm ICEYE bids €41 a share to take Italian rocket maker Avio off the stock market

The bid, tied to satellite maker ICEYE, needs 66.67% of Avio's shares plus clearances from CONSOB, Rome and the European Commission before a takeover can proceed.

18 November 2026

Iris 2 S.p.A. has launched a voluntary tender offer of €41.00 cum dividend per ordinary share of Avio S.p.A, aiming to delist the company from Euronext Milan. The offer covers all of Avio's ordinary shares. Iris 2 is ICEYE's vehicle for the bid. Avio builds launch vehicles, and Leonardo calls it a key supplier to MBDA Italia and MBDA France for missile programmes such as Aster and Camm-Er.

Several approvals still stand between the offer and a takeover. ICEYE must reach 66.67% of Avio's share capital through the offer or through market purchases. Iris 2 will submit the offer document to Italy's market regulator CONSOB, which must approve it before the document is published. The bid also hinges on Italian Golden Power clearance, which Borsaitaliana calls probably the most delicate condition, plus clearances from France and the United Kingdom under their foreign investment rules. It also needs a green light from the European Commission and approval from the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States.

ICEYE has launched eighty-four satellites and runs the world's largest SAR constellation, with more than 1,500 employees, the company says. In June 2026 it completed a capital raise that valued it at over €10 billion. ICEYE says the €41.00 price offers a premium of 21.3% over the previous day's close and 34.8% over the three-month average. Banca Akros wrote that the price takes no account of recent developments in Avio's defence and space businesses.

Avio's board met on 17 November and has started work on its formal response to the offer, the issuer's statement. Avio's free float stands at 60.6% of its share capital, Borsaitaliana reports. Long-term shareholders Leonardo and Advent together hold 24.5% of the capital, with roughly 2% of treasury shares added.

One answer is still missing: Rome's. The Italian government, Il Sole 24 Ore reports, has not yet taken a position on ICEYE's bid for Avio. Part of the reason is procedural, because the transaction has not yet been notified, so the Golden Power assessment cannot start. The broker Equita believes ICEYE at least sounded out the government before launching the offer.
