Europe can train frontier AI. Now pay for the machines to do it again

JUPITER showed a European team can train a model of nearly a trillion parameters on European hardware. The Gigafactory money is waiting, and the finance ministries have yet to sign.

16 November 2026

The question was put in February 2026: could one European team train a frontier model of at least 400 billion parameters, on European machines, in European hands? On 19 June they had their answer, a consortium led by the Italian firm Domyn, with Fraunhofer alongside. And last week that team gave the fuller answer. On 12 November Domyn showed a setup running on JUPITER, the exascale machine in Jülich, at about 952 billion parameters by the consortium's own announcement, close to one trillion. So the engineering works. The money is the part still missing, and that part has a name.

The demonstration did something harder than size. That machine at Jülich is shared with hundreds of scientific projects, and Domyn's engineers could not touch a single piece of its hardware. They rebuilt the training stack around PyTorch and TorchTitan instead, contributed the fixes back to both, and lifted throughput from 90 to about 1,300 tokens per second per GPU, a fourteen-fold jump. The same run used 1,536 GH200 chips in four parallel replicas and Domyn says pre-training is possible in about four months. The configuration, methods and results are published, so other European teams can build on them. Luca Antiga, who announced the JUPITER setup, said Europe's public supercomputers can support frontier-scale training today. The analysts at Kenashe say the parameter count is the least settled part of the story: no weights, no model card, no benchmark, no licence yet, and a trillion-parameter model may matter less than its distilled children, its tooling and its licence terms, which will decide whether EUROPA becomes infrastructure or just a big announcement. That is an argument for finishing in public and shipping the weights and recipes, exactly as the consortium has pledged, and it changes nothing about the binding constraint. The constraint today is compute and capital. Build those.

The French firm Mistral released a roughly trillion-parameter model of its own in October. Size is no longer what Europe lacks. The machines to train on are, and the Commission wants construction of the first Gigafactories to start in 2027.

A model on a shared machine is a proof. What you will see in five years depends on what governments sign this year. The scale is against us and the scale is the reason to move. Epoch AI counted about 72 percent of the world's installed AI compute in the United States between 2022 and June 2026, against about 5 percent for the EU, roughly 1.4 million H100 equivalents, and in the first half of 2026, 78 percent of new arrivals went to America. European investors take part in most early funding rounds for AI start-ups on the continent, but their presence falls to 26 percent in deals above 25 million euros. Sign the Gigafactory contracts and the next EUROPA trains on European machines of its own, with European money behind it. Let the call slip and even a successful open model will run on borrowed hardware.

Who must act now? The Commission and the finance ministries behind it. The Gigafactory call is open and waiting. Commit the construction starts before 2027. Tie the money to InvestAI, the 200 billion euro plan the Commission presented in February 2025, and close the funding gap the Commission itself has measured in those deals. And hold the consortium to its pledge to release the weights and recipes, making publication the checkpoint for the next tranche. What we would be funding is Europe's shared AI infrastructure, open and multilingual in every EU official language, available to businesses, researchers and public institutions. Name the minister, and write. Fund the compute, and fund it in Europe.
