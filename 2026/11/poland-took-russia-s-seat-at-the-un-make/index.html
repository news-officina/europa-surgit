Poland took Russia's seat at the UN. Make that Europe's habit.

One European campaign beat a sixty-year Russian hold on a UN post, by a single vote. EU governments should agree a common candidate before every future contest, so one flag fields it and all of them fight for it.

13 November 2026

Poland just beat Russia at the United Nations, by a single vote, and barely anyone noticed. On 11 November the General Assembly voted, in a secret ballot, on which state would nominate a candidate for the Joint Inspection Unit, the eleven experts who audit how the whole UN system spends and manages itself. Poland took the Eastern European seat with 95 votes, Russia kept 94 and one was withheld, out of 190 cast. Russia had held that seat continuously since the Soviet side did, for sixty years. Now it is gone, because one European government fought. Europe can win at the world's parliament, and it can win on purpose.

The sceptic calls it luck: one vote, quietly backed, proves nothing, and next time Russia wins again. But the arithmetic answers them. The majority required was 95 votes, and Poland reached it. Ambassador Krzysztof Szczerski said the campaign was hard-fought and lasted until the last day, with a meeting at the UN forum on voting day and dozens of messages asking for votes. Poland's permanent representations in New York and Geneva, its embassies and consulates worldwide, ran it with significant support from delegations and offices of the European Union.

Poland built that win by hand. Next time, build it together.

Because from January 2028 it becomes concrete. Jakub Chmielewski, Poland's nominee, takes one of the five vacancies effective that day, on a five-year term running to the end of 2032, renewable once. He has handled the questions he will now audit: a delegate to the General Assembly's Fifth Committee from 2016 to 2021, Vice-Chair of that committee at the seventy-fifth session, and a stint on the UN Committee on Contributions from 2019 to 2021. Chmielewski joins them on a body whose inspectors serve in their personal capacity, chosen for administrative and financial experience, under a statute annexed to General Assembly resolution 31/192. Five years from now, the seat is still his, and the UN's auditors still count a European on it.

Now the demand goes to the governments of the European Union. Instruct your UN delegations in New York and Geneva to agree a common European slate before the next elective contest in the Eastern European group: one candidate, campaigned for by every EU delegation, as Poland's was this month with significant support from the EU's delegations and representative offices. One flag fields the candidate, all of them campaign for it. The obstacle sits in our own capitals, where foreign ministries treat UN seats as prestige trophies under their own flags. The way past is interest, not goodwill. Rotate the agreed candidacies between member states. Each government knows its turn on common backing comes only if it backs another's today. Make sure the next one never comes down to one. Watch for the reports Chmielewski signs when they land. One vote put him there.
