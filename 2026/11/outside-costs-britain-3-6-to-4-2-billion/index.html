Outside costs Britain €3.6 to €4.2 billion a week. Sign the deal.

The compensation clause makes Farage's repeal pledge expensive, and the reset brings Erasmus+ back from January 2027. From the Brussels summit, the next step is a mandate towards association.

18 November 2026

British and European negotiators have agreed the clause that makes the new UK-EU food deal stick, The Times reported this week: either side that walks away pays compensation, both ways. British Prime Minister Andy Burnham, who said on Monday "The Brexit we were given has not worked. We have lost more than we have gained", goes to the Brussels summit on Friday, 20 November, to cut red tape for food exporters, link the two carbon markets and finish a youth experience scheme.

Three things hold it back. Reform UK leader Nigel Farage pledges to scrap the reset deal outright, Kemi Badenoch's Conservatives promise to overturn the alignment rules inside it, and a campaign called Stay Out, chaired by the Vote Leave veteran Oliver Lewis, has put a price on the door: rejoining would cost Britain, it says, £750 million a week, about €900 million. That number is their claim, and it does not survive a first check. John Springford at the Centre for European Reform calls it misleading, because it counts only the gross bill Brussels might charge and ignores the funds that come back. His own accounting runs the other way: being outside has already cost Britain between £3 billion and £3.5 billion a week in lost national income, about €3.6 billion to €4.2 billion, plus £1 billion to £1.3 billion more in forgone tax revenue, and 5.5% of GDP gone. Outside costs at least four times what Stay Out says rejoining would.

Sign the deal in Brussels. Build the association mandate next.

The reset already brings more than either campaign will show you. From January 2027, Britain is back in Erasmus+: students and young people can study, train or take work experience across the union again. Tom Brufatto of the pro-European campaign Best for Britain puts what restoring membership is worth to the economy at £92 billion at the least. The public are ahead of the politicians on that, he says, and want action on the cost of living.

So the ask goes to two desks. The Commission and Burnham should close the deal at the Brussels summit. Then the EU should adopt a negotiating mandate towards a long-term association that can carry a customs union after the next election, for the next British parliament to decide. The compensation clause gets past Farage: repeal now costs British taxpayers. Governments in the Council, put that mandate on the table. And when Stay Out quotes £750 million a week, quote the other number back: the door is open, and it is cheaper to walk through than to stand outside.
